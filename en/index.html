Why Dodo Raskur

Cloud experienceDeploymentPlatformConsole & APISupport

Dodo Raskur is a cloud provider. This guide describes how we present ourselves: how we speak, what we value and how things look, so that a web page, a console screen and a support reply all feel like the same company.

It is a guide to the brand, not a product reference. Regions, limits, prices, SLAs and certifications belong in the service documentation. Quote them from there, never from this guide.

Cloud experience

More than resources

A customer does not come to us for servers. They come to get something running and to stay confident that it keeps running. We call the whole chain between “I need infrastructure” and “it works and I know what it costs” the cloud experience, and that is what we design and write for.

In practice, the cloud experience means:

  • prices are written so that a person can estimate a bill before creating anything;
  • the same thing has the same name in the console, the API and the documentation;
  • an error says what happened and what to do next;
  • nothing important is hidden behind a click, a sales call or a footnote.

Deployment

From idea to running workload

Getting started is the first real test of a cloud provider. We write and design it so that the path from signing up to a first running workload is short, visible and reversible.

  • name each step plainly and say how many remain;
  • state defaults, and say when a default affects cost or security;
  • show what is about to be created, and what it costs, before the customer confirms;
  • make status visible while something is happening, and say when it is done.

Platform

A platform you can trust

We aim to be consistent everywhere. Someone who learns one Dodo Raskur service should be able to guess how the next one works: the same vocabulary, the same order of steps, the same behaviour when something goes wrong.

Predictable, not surprising

Limits, quotas and side effects are stated where the customer makes the decision. If an action cannot be undone, the interface says so before it happens.

Clear and uncluttered

Every screen and page should make the next step obvious. Remove what does not help the customer decide or act.

Console & API

One product, two doors

People reach Dodo Raskur through the console, through the API and command-line tools, and through the documentation. Treat them as one product: the same terms, the same logic, the same wording in errors.

  • Honest data. Examples and screenshots show realistic data and real behaviour, not an idealised version of it.
  • Examples that run. A code sample in the documentation should work as written.
  • Accessible by default. Interfaces work with a keyboard and a screen reader, keep text readable, and respect reduced-motion settings.

Support

“Zero non-responsiveness” principle

Things go wrong in any complex system, and we will make mistakes too. What the customer remembers is how we respond.

The principle we write and design to: every message gets an answer, even if the answer is “we don’t know yet, here is when we will”. A person who reaches out should find a way to a human, not a wall of automated replies.

Feedback is not a nuisance to be managed. We aim to read it wherever customers leave it, and treat a repeated complaint as a defect in the product or in this guide.

Manifesto

Every day our customers deal with numerous tasks: build, ship, fix, scale, migrate, explain to colleagues. Infrastructure is rarely the thing they actually want to think about. We don’t know exactly what each of them is building. What we do know is how to make the infrastructure part a little easier, a little clearer and a little less stressful. When somebody needs compute to run something, a place to keep data, or just a straight answer, they have Dodo Raskur.

We’ll take care of the infrastructure while you’re taking care of what matters most!

Vision

We look to the future with optimism and believe in a world full of builders who care.

Builders are the people who make things work: they read the documentation, ask precise questions and notice when something is off. They are proactive, attentive and demanding. These are the people who care.

When something goes wrong, a builder writes to support, not out of anger, but to make sure it won’t happen again, to them or to anyone else. In a way they are our partners. We show them openness and trust, and they give us the specific, honest feedback that makes the service better.

Builders are the ones who give meaning to what we do. They turn infrastructure into products that matter to their own customers, and that makes Dodo Raskur matter too.

We want every customer to be treated well, whatever the size of their project. Still, it is the builders who care that we consider the core of our audience, and our mission is aimed at them.

Mission

We want customers to become more curious and more demanding. In other words, we want to see as many builders who care as possible. How can we have an impact on this? We have a way.

To encourage curiosity,

we are open

We want to explain how our services work, what they cost and where their limits are. We listen carefully to criticism so that we can change for the better, and we say so when we get something wrong.

By our own example we show that openness and attentiveness make technology a more comfortable and reliable place to work.

To enable demanding behaviour,

we build quality services

We aim for services that are clear, dependable and honest about what they do. The better the tools, the more customers can reasonably ask of them, and of us.

Whatever we release, we want it to be an example of attentiveness and of a responsible attitude to the customer’s work and data.

Our mission is

to build a culture of openness and make the cloud clearer to work with, so that there are more builders who care out there

Values

AccessibilityAttentivenessTrustOpennessQuality

What are “values”?

Is it something that we see as important and strive for? Or is it something that customers appreciate in us, what they choose us for and single us out from others?

It may seem like two different questions. Our aim is to have the same answer to both.

If we invest in a quality, and customers notice and value it, then we don’t need to pretend. We can just be ourselves.

Below are five values and what each means for how we write, design and behave. They describe intent and working rules, not measured results.

Accessibility

The idea of accessibility runs through the whole way we present ourselves. We strive to be accessible to every customer, in the broadest sense:

  • Clarity. Plain language first. Explain a term the first time it appears, and avoid jargon where an ordinary word works.
  • Price transparency. A customer should be able to find out what something costs without talking to anyone.
  • Interface accessibility. Keyboard navigation, screen reader support, sufficient contrast and respect for reduced-motion settings are baseline requirements, not extras.
  • Reach. Pages and documentation should work on modest devices and connections, and in the customer’s own language where we can offer it.

Attentiveness

We are fundamentally attentive. Attentiveness improves relationships among people and helps build trust.

We are attentive:

  • in the console: we think about what the customer is trying to do at this very moment;
  • in support: we respond to every request, and when something goes wrong we say so and fix it;
  • in public: we read every comment and suggestion, and we respond to people;
  • with partners and the team: we value everyone and take everyone’s interests into account.

It is important that attentiveness is fundamental. Not situational, on the occasion of a good mood, but unconditional.

Trust

Customers trust us with their work and their data. That is the key relationship in a cloud business, and everything we write and design should earn it.

In practice:

  • We say what we do. Descriptions match behaviour. If a service has a limit, we state it.
  • We don’t surprise customers. Costs, changes and deprecations are announced where the customer will see them.
  • We take responsibility. When something is our fault, we say so plainly and explain what we are doing about it.
  • We trust customers back. Interfaces and policies assume good faith and don’t treat the customer as a suspect.

Openness

For us, openness is:

  • A quality instrument. When things are in plain sight, it is easier to check them against our standards.
  • An improvement driver. We want signals from outside, and we use them.
  • Transparency. Prices, limits and changes are written down where customers can read them.
  • Honesty. We tell it as it is. No tricks and no text in a minuscule font. If we are wrong, we don’t hide mistakes, we recognise them and draw conclusions.
  • Impartiality. We treat people with respect regardless of who they are or how big their project is.

Quality

Customers can tell the difference between clear and confusing, fast and slow, dependable and fragile. So we take quality seriously in everything that customers can see.

  • Clear writing. Short sentences, concrete nouns, no filler. Say what the thing is and what to do with it.
  • Consistent naming. One name per concept, used everywhere.
  • Correct examples. Code, commands and numbers shown to customers are checked before publication.
  • Care in the details. Spelling, alignment, contrast and loading states all count.

Readable text on colour

Contrast ratios (WCAG) for common pairings. Normal-size text needs at least 4.5:1.

  • Black on orange #FF6900: 7.3:1. Use black text on orange.
  • White on orange #FF6900: 2.9:1. Fails even the 3:1 large-text threshold. Do not set text white on orange; the logo files are the only exception.
  • White on deep orange #E65300: 3.8:1. Large text only.
  • White on plum #53284F: 11.8:1. Safe for any size.
  • Orange #FF6900 on white: 2.9:1. Not for body text or thin links.

Illustration

Dodo Raskur illustrations are drawn with a confident black outline and flat orange fills, with a friendly, slightly humorous character. They are used to explain and to add warmth, never to decorate for the sake of it.

  • Keep the line weight and the palette consistent across a set.
  • Prefer one clear idea per illustration.
  • Illustrations support the text; they must not carry information that is missing from it. Give meaningful images descriptive alt text.

The full illustration reference, with examples, is on its own page: Illustration guide.

Digital

For a cloud provider, the digital surface is the product: the console, the documentation and the website are where nearly every customer meets us.

  • Orange is for action. Use it for the primary action on a screen and for key highlights. A screen with orange everywhere has no primary action.
  • Text first. Dense technical content is set in Pragmatica on white, in black or near-black, with enough spacing to scan.
  • Focus is visible. Every interactive element has a clear focus state and a hit area large enough for touch.
  • Motion is quiet. Use animation to show a change of state, keep it short, and honour reduced-motion settings.
  • States are designed. Empty, loading, error and success states get the same care as the default state.

This guide will keep changing. If something here is unclear, missing or out of date, treat that as a defect in the guide and fix it.
